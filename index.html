Words we will use

VectorA list of numbers that describes an item (a photo, a sentence, a song).
Query (Q)What we search with, e.g. a photo we want similar photos for.
DistanceHow different two vectors are. Small distance = very similar.
Top-KThe K best matches found so far (e.g. best 10).
Bar to beat (TopK_W)Distance of the worst of those K. A new item must be closer than this to get in.
RecallShare of the true best answers we found. 100% = perfect.
Cluster & centerA cluster is a group of similar vectors. Its center is the “average” point of the group.
Problem · Block 1 of 4

Vector search

Text, images and audio are turned into vectors (lists of numbers) so a computer can compare them. Similar items get similar numbers. When we search (the query, Q), we want the stored vectors closest to it.

Scale: millions to billions of vectors, hundreds of dimensions, answers needed in milliseconds.

Problem · Block 2 of 4 (a)

Cluster-based index: the idea

Think of a library. Instead of checking every book, books are grouped on shelves by topic.

Shelf = cluster. We only walk to the nearest shelf(s), so most books are never touched.

Problem · Block 2 of 4 (b)

How it works, step by step

1 · Build (once)Group similar vectors; each group gets a center.
2 · Pick clustersCompare Q with the few centers only. Keep the nprobe nearest clusters.
3 · Search insideMeasure Q against every vector in those clusters. Keep the best K.
Problem · Block 3 of 4

Where does the time go?

Measuring the distance from Q to one vector means working through all its numbers (say 128). Doing that for millions of vectors is slow.

Share of query time at 99% recall

Computing vector distances ≈ 98–99%
Everything else ≈ 1–2%
Too coarseEvery vector in a selected cluster is treated the same, near the query or far from it.
StaticAs better results are found, the bar to enter the top-K rises, but nothing exploits that.
Problem · Block 4 of 4

The challenge

Can we skip distance calculations without losing accuracy?

Idea: a simple rule from geometry, the triangle inequality (next slide), lets us estimate a distance without measuring it.

Curse of dimensionalityPoints sit on a thin shell, and random vectors are nearly perpendicular, so bounds are loose.
OverheadExtra computation at query time can cost more than the pruning saves.

Tribase answers both: precompute offline, prune with distance and angle online.

The key trick

Triangle inequality, in plain words

Going straight is never longer than a detour. Home→Shop is 5 km and Shop→School is 1 km, so Home→School must be between 4 and 6 km, without walking it.

Home = the query Q
Shop = a known landmark (e.g. a cluster center)
School = an item we hope to skip

If even the smallest possible distance is above the bar to beat, the item cannot make the top-K. Skip it, safely.

How Tribase works · Part 2 of 4

Center-distance pruning

Known: Q to center (already measured to pick clusters) and P to center (saved in training). Unknown: Q to P.

Q to P ≥ 10 − 3 = 7, which is more than 5. Skip P!

Rule: Q-to-center minus P-to-center > bar to beat ⇒ skip. Very cheap to check.

How Tribase works · Part 3 of 4

subNN distance pruning

“subNN” = each item’s saved nearest neighbors. A is already measured and is far from Q. B is one of A’s neighbors.

Q to B ≥ 12 − 2 = 10, more than 5. Skip B!

Intuition: if A is far away and B sits right next to A, B is far too. Catches items the center rule misses.

How Tribase works · Part 4 of 4

subNN angle pruning

Distance is not the only clue. Direction also works.

From the center, only items pointing toward Q (inside the cone) can be close to Q. Items pointing elsewhere are skipped.

We measure each item as a direction from its center, so items spread out instead of all looking alike. Angles also obey a triangle rule, which keeps this safe.

Works well in high dimensions but needs a few extra multiplications.

Takeaways

Three things to remember

  1. Distance computation dominates cluster-based search, so pruning it pays off.
  2. Triangle inequalities on distance and angle prune safely, and precomputing offline keeps query overhead low.
  3. Up to 10× faster than Faiss with lossless results, in exchange for more build time and memory.
